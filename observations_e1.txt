When memory management was turned OFF, all 10 processes completed with minimal delays, and their response, running, and waiting times were relatively small because no page faults occurred and every instruction executed without interruption. 
However, when memory management was turned ON, the timings were noticeably impacted: processes with GOOD memory behavior showed only a small increase in running and turnaround times, whereas BAD processes demonstrated significant slowdowns due to frequent page faults. 
Overall, enabling virtual memory increased total execution time for the system, with the impact being strongly dependent on the process’ memory behavior — good processes were only mildly affected, while bad processes became substantially slower due to repeated page loading and replacement overhead.
